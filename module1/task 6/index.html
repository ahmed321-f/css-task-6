CSS Combinators

A combinator explains the relationship between two selectors.

1. Descendant Selector (space)

Syntax: div p { } — selects every p inside a div, at any depth.

I am a direct child p inside div.

I am a nested p inside div → section. I am selected too.

I am outside the div, so I am not selected.

2. Child Selector (>)

Syntax: ul > li { } — selects only direct children.

  • Direct child (selected)
  • Direct child (selected)
    1. Grandchild (not selected)
    2. Grandchild (not selected)

3. Adjacent Sibling Selector (+)

Syntax: h3 + p { } — selects the first p placed immediately after an h3.

I come right after the h3 (selected).

I am the second p (not selected).

I am the third p (not selected).

4. General Sibling Selector (~)

Syntax: h4 ~ span { } — selects all span siblings that come after an h4.

A paragraph in between (ignored)

Last span (selected)

5. Selector List (comma)

Syntax: h5, em { } — applies one style to several selectors. Strictly it is a grouping, not a combinator, but it is often studied with them.
